# Modules — Tenure spans and operator succession

The tenure-span subject area, which since #189 (AR-14) spans two layers:

- **Layer 2, `packages/clearinghouse-domain-legislative/`** — the *engine*:
  `terms.py` (biennium arithmetic), `tenure_spans.py` (the merged-span builder),
  `operator_overlay.py` (the succession overlay) and `seat_clipping.py`.
  Jurisdiction-agnostic; a sibling jurisdiction can take these without a WSL SOAP client.
- **Layer 3, `packages/usa-wa-adapter-legislature/`** — the WSL *inputs*: the
  operator-event and committee-succession stores and CLIs, roster hygiene and the pure
  projectors. The ingest half of the same package is in
  [MODULES-LEGISLATURE.md](MODULES-LEGISLATURE.md).

The builders that compose them are the #302 pipeline's conformed models
(`usa_wa_pipeline.conformed.spans`, `.house`) — [PIPELINE-CONFORMED.md](PIPELINE-CONFORMED.md).
The Postgres span emitter, the Phase-B span builders, the succession invariants and the
span-collapse migrations were deleted in #412 PR F.

`sponsors/roster_hygiene.py` deliberately stayed in Layer 3 against the #189 sketch: every
function it exports takes a WSL `GetSponsors` row (`Id`/`FirstName`/`LastName`/
`Name`/`Agency`/`District`), so promoting it would have dragged one adapter's wire
shape into the domain model. It is a source-specific hygiene rule, not span
machinery. Its consumer is the pipeline, which may import an adapter (never a transport).

```
      operator_overlay.py *(Layer 2 since #189)* — the **pure** span-boundary overlay (#107): `apply_operator_events(spans, events, *, current_biennium, owned_kinds, movers_by_biennium=None, context_spans=(), applied_departures=None)` runs after `build_tenure_spans` in ALL **four** conformed span families — sponsor, committee and roster in `usa_wa_pipeline.conformed.spans`, House in `usa_wa_pipeline.conformed.house`. The roster family is the only one that owns a span below the 1991 floor, so the roster's own dated boundaries — every one of them pre-1991 — apply there or nowhere. `departed` closes every open member span at the date **except one the member re-enters** (usa-wa#267): `build_tenure_spans` merges contiguous biennia into a single span, so for a member who left and later returned that one row covers both tenures and closing it at the departure discards the second — Elmer Huntley resigned 1965-03-26 and was appointed to Senate LD9 on 1967-04-24, and the truncation left him holding a seat with no party span under it for five years (2,078 days, caught in prod by the party-coverage check). The guard is narrow: only a span whose *start* post-dates the event counts as a re-entry, so a seat already running at the date — exactly what a death ends — still closes. The second exception is a tenure a `seated` opened **at the same instant** (#363): seat-scoped events apply first, so on a same-day chamber move the sweep would close the seat the member moved INTO at `valid_to = max(d, d)` — zero length, invisible to every gate — which is how Derek Stanford lost 18 months of Senate tenure. The member did not leave, they moved; the seat they moved OUT of still closes (`operator_departed_spares_same_instant_seating`). Since #267 the span is **split** rather than skipped: close the first tenure at the departure, reopen a second at the return, keyed on the return's biennium so its `source_id` is stable across re-drives (`operator_departed_span_split_at_return`). A return inside the departure's own biennium would key the tail to the *same* `source_id`, so that one leaves the tenure whole and logs `operator_departed_split_same_biennium` rather than emit two spans under one key. Seat-scoped events apply **before** person-scoped ones so the split reads a settled `valid_from` — and, because that ordering removes the accidental protection the old one gave (a `departed` closed the window before a spurious later `seated` could match it), only the **earliest** `seated` dates a given span: a second one matching an already-dated tenure is logged `operator_seated_tenure_already_dated` and skipped. Per span, not per seat — a gap-and-return member holds one seat twice and each tenure takes its own seating. Without it a successor's seating mis-resolved onto the incumbent re-dated Christine Rolfes' twelve-year Senate tenure to its final week — Huntley's party tenure reopens at his 1967-04-24 swearing-in, not the biennium floor. `context_spans` carries the member's spans another builder owns, read-only, so the split is not blind across the builder seam (Pike returned to a House seat while her party span is the sponsor family's); `vacated` closes the one named seat (date inside its window); `seated` sets the named seat's `valid_from` — but only on a tenure it actually **starts** (usa-wa#272): the span's `start_biennium` must begin at or after the event's own biennium, else `operator_seated_does_not_start_tenure` and no match. Window-containment alone is not enough, because `build_tenure_spans` merges contiguous biennia and a thirteen-year tenure's window holds almost any date in it — a successor's seating then re-dates the incumbent's span to its final weeks (member 630's House tenure keyed 2003-04, recorded as six weeks in 2016; likewise `233:chamber-senate:22:1985-86` at 1991-04-18). The bound is *at or after* rather than equality because a span starting one biennium late is the very defect the overlay corrects — Graham Hunt, appointed 2014-01-17, has only `ld-2-position-1:2015-16`. Its window opens 2015-01-01, so a seating **no window holds** falls back to the seat's span opening on the **next** biennium's floor (usa-wa#282, `_opens_after_seating`): one biennium only, onto a *quantized* start only; `start_biennium` (so the key) unchanged. A refused seating still falls through to synthesis, the correct home for one the wire built no tenure for. — or **synthesizes** the open span if the wire built none (a biennium-floor appointee), but **only for a current-biennium `effective_date`** (#119): a `seated` dated outside `current_biennium` with no built span is a historical appointee, and synthesizing would mint a bogus current-biennium open seat for a departed member (corrupting the record + tripping the chamber-count gate) — the build covers every biennium, so their span is built and the event matches it instead; else skip + log `operator_seated_no_span_out_of_biennium`. `owned_kinds` scopes seat-events to the kinds this builder produces (a `seated chamber-house` is the House family's, not the sponsor's). An operator-touched **non-mover** is exempt from the #105 stale exclusion so their span is built for the overlay to date — but the exemption is **biennium-scoped** (`stale_exempt_members`, #145 CR): a member is exempt only through their **latest event biennium**, so a committee-stale member in a LATER biennium is a genuine post-event ghost (an election loss — O'Ban's Senate seat, 2021-22) whose cumulative-wire ghost must NOT extend their span (a global exemption stretched such spans into a spurious later-biennium duplicate). An operator-touched **mover** is deliberately NOT re-included (re-including one re-runs the #103 elimination and splits the House family into superseded duplicates — the reverted 2013-14 tranche, #145). Instead `vacated` **synthesizes** the mover's CLOSED `[biennium-floor→date]` House tenure, gated on the per-biennium mover signal (`movers_by_biennium`, from `usa_wa_facts_seats.pdc.matching.house_mover_ids`) — in a pre-pass before any event applies (usa-wa#461), so the mover's earlier `seated` dates the span it mints instead of matching nothing and leaving it on the floor (member 15814, seated 2011-01-05). It mints each span key **at most once**, a built span's key included: a window check alone would mint a second `…:<biennium>` row beside an earlier vacate's (or beside a built span starting after the date), two rows under one `span_key`; the later `vacated` is a logged `operator_vacated_no_span` instead; closed-synth is safe where the #119 *open*-synth guard isn't — a closed historical span can't inflate the current open-chamber count. Since #289 the overlay **rejoins party spans on the way out** (`merge_party_continuity`): the split above is still load-bearing — without it a `departed` truncates the party tenure and nothing reopens it, which is the Pike case the context spans exist for — but the observable end state for `party` is one span across the gap, because a break in elected service is evidence about a seat and not about an affiliation. Seat and committee spans are untouched, and an attested span of a *different* party still breaks the run. A `departed` miss is logged once per conformed WSL family (#466, `log_departure_misses`); within 31 days of the last span end it is `operator_departed_already_closed`. Pure + idempotent — the nightly re-drives every builder, so the overlay re-applies each run and the wire can never win back a corrected span
      operators/store.py — the operator-attestation store (#107): `record_operator_event` buffers the serialized event (canonical JSON) on the caller's required `raw` — that raw-store body is the attestation's only provenance, covered by the weekly integrity sweep — and upserts the projection row in `registry.operator_events` by its natural key; `supersede_event` appends a correction + stamps the prior row's `superseded_by_id` — and since #363 may **reclassify** the boundary within `ENDING_KINDS` (`departed` ↔ `vacated` are two readings of one annotation) but never turn an ending into a beginning; `current_events` (non-superseded only). The pipeline reads the table through `usa_wa_pipeline.operator_read`, never this module (an import-linter contract forbids it)
      operators/raw.py — operator attestations into the #304 raw store (#412 PR A): `PendingAttestations` buffers each attestation body a store writes and `flush()`es it as one `usa_wa_operator` run **after** the caller commits, so a rolled-back write (`--dry-run`, a validation failure) leaves no manifest and no object. Same `resource_id`, `urn:usa-wa-operator:` url and content type as the #305 export, so the source reads as one ledger across the cutover; a body identical to its resource's newest record is not re-recorded. `archive_after_commit` is the CLIs' post-commit step: a flush that fails after the commit returns `False` and the run reports degraded, naming the recovery (record the event again — idempotent, so the bytes land). Both CLIs flush through it; the committee-succession store shares it
      operators/cli.py — operator-succession **CLI** (#107, `python -m usa_wa_adapter_legislature.operators.cli`): the live interjection surface. Direct-arg OR `--file` JSON batch OR `--supersede <id>` date-correction; `--list` inspects; `--dry-run` rolls back. Validates kind/reason/seat shape AND that `member_id` is a registered person — the registry key `usa_wa_legislature:<id>` — before writing (a typo would otherwise be a silent no-op overlay). App-role DML (shell access is the trust boundary); exit 2 on a validation failure. The nightly applies each event on its next build
      committees/succession_store.py / committees/succession_cli.py — the committee-lineage twin (usa-wa#124): `record_succession_event` / `supersede_event` / `current_events` / `superseded_events` over `registry.committee_succession_events`, same `raw` buffer and append-only supersede; the CLI (`python -m usa_wa_adapter_legislature.committees.succession_cli`) validates that **both** `--subject` and `--linked` are registered `usa_wa_legislature` committee orgs. Published nightly as the `org_lineage` dataset (#447)
      membership/projector.py — committee-roster → `Observation` projection (#82), pure: kind=`committee`, discriminator = the committee's stable WSL `Id` (a re-keyed committee is a different committee, so its membership is a new span); one observation per named member per (committee, biennium). The pipeline's conformed committee family builds from it
      sponsors/projector.py — `build_sponsor_observations` (#78), pure: archived `GetSponsors` rows → a major-party `party` observation per named row + a `chamber-senate` seat observation per Senate row, honouring an `exclude_ids_by_biennium` set (the hygiene below). The pipeline's conformed sponsor family; the House seat is `usa_wa_facts_seats.house`'s
      sponsors/roster_hygiene.py — sponsor-wire hygiene (#105 (b)), pure: `stale_member_ids` — a fully-named `GetSponsors` row whose member is absent from that biennium's committee-roster archive is a departed-member ghost (Kilduff left Dec 2020, named through 2025-26; Senn/Nguyen resigned Jan 2025 — both chambers), excluded before span projection so its party/seat spans end at the real departure boundary. Committee presence is the corroborating signal (a sitting member — incl. a fresh appointee — is always committee-active; verified 1999-00→2025-26). Guarded by a coverage floor (`STALE_MIN_COVERAGE_DEFAULT` 0.9, the #44/#56 pattern): a biennium whose committee cohort names <90% of the named rows skips the exclusion (`stale_exclusion_skipped_low_coverage`) — a thin/absent archive (pre-1999-00) never reads as mass departure. **Tail rule** (`stale_exclusions_by_biennium`, the consumer entry point): a candidate is excluded only when committee-absent in that biennium AND every later one — later presence marks an archive *gap*, not a departure (the audit's Shewmake case: a sitting 2019-2022 member missing by Id+name from every archived House-era roster, committee-present after her Senate move → `stale_exclusion_rescued_by_later_presence`), and exclusions therefore only trim tenure tails — never a mid-tenure hole, so no span splits/superseded rows. Consumed by the pipeline's sponsor (`conformed.spans`) and House (`conformed.house`) families, both under `stale_min_coverage`. The sibling **mover exclusion** (#105 (a)) lives in `usa_wa_facts_seats.pdc.matching.build_house_roster`
      sponsors/artifacts.py — curated member-artifact denylist (#144), pure: `ARTIFACT_EXCLUSIONS_BY_BIENNIUM` (`{biennium: {member_id}}`) + `with_artifact_exclusions` — a manually-curated, roster-evidenced set of spurious WSL `GetSponsors` appearances (a member the wire names in a biennium they did NOT serve). Unioned into the `exclude_ids_by_biennium` set `build_sponsor_observations` honours (in `conformed.spans`, **after** the operator-exemption subtraction so it's a hard exclusion), so no rebuild re-derives the phantom span. Distinct from `sponsors.roster_hygiene` (data-driven, committee-corroborated departed-ghost) — an artifact here is a fully-formed committee-present wire row no automatic signal catches. First entry: `("2001-02", "481")` = **John Wynne** LD39 Senate chamber-conflation artifact (roster: House-39 only, 1991; Val Stevens held LD39 Senate 1997-2012 continuously — his real 1991-92 party span is untouched)
```
